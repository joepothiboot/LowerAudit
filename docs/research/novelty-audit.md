## 1. The two supplied pointers

### Brandner, ASAP 2010 (peer-reviewed)

F. Brandner, *Completeness of automatically generated instruction selectors*, ASAP 2010,
pp. 175–182, [doi:10.1109/ASAP.2010.5540994](https://doi.org/10.1109/ASAP.2010.5540994).
Read: abstract and bibliographic records ([DTU Orbit](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/completeness-of-automatically-generated-instruction-selectors/)).
The [HAL copy](https://hal-ens-lyon.archives-ouvertes.fr/ensl-00531354v1) was bot-blocked for this
session. **Full text not yet read.**

1. **Problem:** is a tree-pattern instruction selector, generated from a processor model, complete?
2. **Input model:** selector specifications. "Traditional approaches for completeness tests cannot
   represent dynamic checks that may disable certain matching rules". Brandner applies *terminal
   splitting* to make those checks explicit, then runs "a traditional completeness test".
3. **Real compiler or formal rules?** Formal rules. The check uses tree automata on the specification.
4. **Uncovered inputs?** Yes. "If the test fails, counter examples are presented".
5. **Unused patterns?** Not stated in the abstract.
6. **Finite bound or all-ranks result?** No rank parameter. Tree-automata emptiness is a decision
   procedure, not a cutoff.
7. **What this project adds:** execution of real, hand-written C++ conversions whose guards cannot
   be turned into tree automata; a declared validity oracle; and a rank parameter.
8. **Distinct?** In method, yes. The *question* (is every input covered, with counterexamples) is
   Brandner's and Emmelmann's. Our per-dimension abstraction letters are terminal splitting applied
   to shapes, and must be credited as such.

### ACT, OOPSLA 2026 (peer-reviewed, accepted)

D. Jain, A. Pardeshi, M. Frigo, K. Khulbe, K. Patel, S. Lochan, J. Arora, C. Mendis,
*Automatically Generating ML Compiler Backends from Tensor Accelerator ISA Descriptions*,
OOPSLA 2026 ([program page](https://2026.splashcon.org/details/oopsla-2026/83/Automatically-Generating-ML-Compiler-Backends-from-Tensor-Accelerator-ISA-Description)).
Preprint [arXiv:2510.09932](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09932): v1 (11 Oct 2025) was titled
"ACT: Automatically Generating Compiler Backends from Tensor Accelerator ISA Descriptions"; v2
(18 Aug 2026) is marked "Accepted at OOPSLA 2026". Code: [act-compiler/act](https://github.com/act-compiler/act).
Read: abstracts of v1 and v2, §3.5, and Theorem 1 with its lemma structure in v1's HTML; the
last ~38k characters were not read. The v1 abstract says "We prove that compiler backends
generated by ACT are sound and complete"; the v2 abstract does not repeat the sentence.

1. **Problem:** generate a backend (instruction selection by equality saturation, memory
   allocation by constraint programming) from an ISA description, integrated with XLA.
2. **Input model:** concrete tensor computation graphs. Completeness (§3.5): "the compilation is
   successful for all input graphs that have an equivalent assembly code". Equivalence is
   relative to a rewrite set ℛ that is taken as axioms.
3. **Real compiler or formal rules?** It *generates* a new backend. It does not audit an existing one.
4. **Uncovered inputs?** Completeness is proved by construction (Theorem 1, ∀ ISA). It depends on
   ℛ, on an extraction budget, and on the completeness of the CP-SAT solver.
5. **Unused patterns?** None seen.
6. **Finite bound or all-ranks result?** None seen over shapes or ranks. The bounds in the paper are
   per graph (orderings, extraction budget).
7. **What this project adds:** an audit of *existing hand-written* conversions, and an all-rank
   statement from finite checks.
8. **Distinct?** Yes, but ACT removes "first completeness guarantee for accelerator lowering" from
   anything we might claim.

## 3. Verdict (provisional)

Removed from the novelty claim:

- completeness checking of lowering or selection rules (Emmelmann, Brandner, sufficient completeness);
- reporting rules that never fire (GlobalISel rule coverage);
- completeness guarantees for accelerator backends (ACT);
- generating MLIR test inputs (the fuzzers);
- cutoff theorems as mathematics (parameterized verification).

Possibly open (nothing published found in this search):

1. Exhaustive, oracle-judged coverage checking of **real, hand-written MLIR conversion pattern
   sets** over a declared domain.
2. A sufficient-rank theorem for tensor lowering whose hypotheses are **discharged against the
   conversion's source**. It turns finite checks into all-rank coverage claims, and into *proofs*
   that a pattern is unreachable in D.

The emergent capability is (2) applied to (1): sound "unreachable in D" and "covered at every rank"
statements about real conversions. Neither the testing tools nor the formal checkers above
deliver that alone.

Caveats:

- Theorem 1's mathematics is elementary automata theory. The contribution would be the
  abstraction, the method for discharging obligations, and the empirical results, not new math.
- The claim is only interesting if the target's rank logic is non-trivial (R1). Hypothesis, not run: on `ttir.prod` the logic found so far is one verifier threshold plus a mark summary, so this caveat still applies.
- "Oracle-judged" now means judged by the TTIR verifiers only (D-014), not by a spec. Whether that weakens item 1 above is unasked (OQ-8).
- The CLAUDE.md phrase "as black boxes" is narrowed by D-006: execution is black-box, the rank
  obligations are white-box.
